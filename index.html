An AI startup · Data ontology

Hard problems can be solved when people understand their data. Thothica helps them do that by building ontologies and AI-native software.

Thothica is an AI startup. We build ontologies, and we build software on top of them.

An ontology is a precise written record of what an organization's data means. It states what each item is, how the items relate to one another, and who is responsible for each decision.

When software runs on an ontology, your staff and your AI systems work from the same definitions, and your organization no longer depends on only a few experienced people to interpret its data.

You can then solve, at scale, the problems those people used to solve one at a time.

What we build

  1. 1

    We integrate AI layers into legacy software. Your existing system stays exactly as it is. We read its database and write down what its tables and columns mean in the words your staff actually use, and the layer sits on top of that.

    Your staff can then ask questions in plain language instead of opening many screens and exporting spreadsheets. Because the layer answers from a written record rather than guessing, every number it reports can be traced back to the data it came from.

  2. 2

    We build approval systems for governments. Before we write any software, we write down what each scheme actually requires: which document proves which condition, which officer signs at which stage, and what makes an application complete.

    The software then checks every application against that record. The applicant sees exactly what is missing. The official sees which files are ready for a decision, and which are stuck and why. Approvals move faster because nobody has to work out what a case needs.

  3. 3

    We built the AI layer that now runs on five state government portals, each built on a different technology. For each portal we first wrote down what its data measures and the ways it can be broken down: by district, by scheme, by month, by status.

    An official can then ask a question in plain language and get an answer taken from that portal's own records, with every figure traceable back to the rows it came from. The layer only reads. It cannot change anything in the system it sits on.

  4. 4

    For a think tank, we rebuilt an archive of more than 1,000 historical works. What they had was more than 2,000 PDFs and scattered web pages on a slow, outdated website.

    The ontology was the real work. We identified every thinker in the collection and matched their names across scripts and spellings, so that one person stays one person however a byline was printed. We recorded what kind of document each item is, whether a speech, a pamphlet, a book or a periodical article. We recorded the subjects each thinker wrote on. And we recorded how the items relate to one another: who wrote what, who replied to whom, and what one thinker wrote about another.

    On top of that we built a content management system designed around this structure, and a fast digital library. Researchers can search it in five languages, and every answer points back to the original document.

  5. 5

    We run Falsafa.ai, our open-source library of the world's philosophical, classical and religious texts. It holds more than 2,000 works, divided into more than 1.3 million passages that can each be quoted and cited exactly.

    On top of the library we build the Atlas, a map of the collection drawn from the texts themselves. It records the people, ideas, places, groups, events and objects that appear across twenty-five centuries of writing. It also records every time one text cites another, and how: whether the later author treated the earlier one as an authority, agreed with it, extended it, or argued against it.

    Every entry is tied to a paragraph you can read in place. The software that builds the Atlas is not allowed to write quotations. It can only point at paragraphs, and the words are attached afterwards from the source text, so an invented quotation is impossible.

  6. 6

    We produce research-based graphic stories at scale. We first turn the source material, which may be books, long interviews or video, into a structured research library where every chapter is indexed and every fact keeps a record of where it came from.

    Scripts are drafted from that library, and every line carries a citation back to the exact source line. A check resolves each citation before the script goes forward, so nothing unsourced reaches the page. Our software then keeps the characters and the drawing style consistent from page to page, through to print-ready files.
